I remember, in middle school, 9th graders would always go on a trip to the UK.
When it was our time, when WE were the 9th graders or soon to be 9th grader, acts of terrorism blew kind of everywhere, therefore trips to the UK were forbidden and trips cancelled. I was very disappointed, as going the United Kingdom was like a dream., I always dreamt going there, being surrounded by English. During 

Then one day, we got news on the lockdowns. 
Borders were finally re-opened, School trips were re-authorized.
We then learned that the trip to the United Kingdom was accepted, I was over the moon, finally ! 
Soon enough, we were planning groups for the host families, getting paper to survive in a total unknown land for us.

D-day, its THE day, I was as much happy and excited as I was stressed. I went to Spain when I was younger, but Spain is linked to France whereas the UK, we had to cross a big chunk of salted water called the Ocean.
We first sailed the seven seas, well, it felt like we did. At the start, we all went to discover the inside of the Ferry (we weren't authorized to go out). It was like if we went to another world, I thought it would be very basic, very limited, but it was the opposite. There were big shops, kind of prestigious jewellery stores and a dance floor.
Then time to sleep. We slept in a room full seats and let me tell you, sleeping on a boat, on rock hard seat, is very hard. 
I recall that night, the science teacher was by the window kind of like a mad scientist watching the thunder, excited at each strike like a puppy seeing his owner enter the house then nothing, I must have fallen asleep, rocked by the water and the sweet occasional flashing lights.
